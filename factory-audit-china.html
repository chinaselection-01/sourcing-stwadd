A factory audit is the cheapest hour you will ever spend on a China order

A full audit costs a few hundred dollars and takes a day. Skipping it risks a container that cannot be sold, a certification that turns out to belong to another product, or a supplier who was never going to make what you ordered. It is the best-value insurance in importing.

Before the audit: what to request

Send the list a week ahead. An audit that arrives unannounced mostly measures how the factory performs when nervous, which is not the same as how it performs on your production run.

Request: the business licence, the quality management certificate if they hold ISO 9001, export records or bills of lading for the last twelve months, a list of current customers in your market, and photos of the line you are asking about with dates from the last week.

The photos are the useful part. A factory with nothing to show you either has nothing to show you, or will not tell you why.

On the floor: what to look at

Capacity that matches the claim. Count the lines. Ask how many shifts run. Then ask what the schedule looks like for the next eight weeks. A factory claiming monthly capacity well beyond what its lines can produce in three shifts is telling you its number is theoretical.

Quality control that is actually happening. Look for an inspection station, not just a promise. Ask to see records from the previous order: how many units were rejected last month and what was done about it. A factory with zero rejects on a production line is either very good or not recording.

Traceability. Pick an item on the shelf and ask where the steel came from. A supplier who cannot name a mill or trace a material certificate has a traceability problem that will become your problem when a regulator or a major retailer asks.

Storage and handling. Finished goods stored on the floor, stacked against a wall with no dunnage, or mixed without labels will produce the dented and mismatched units that generate your complaint.

Labour and social compliance

If you supply a market that audits social compliance, this is the part that will disqualify a factory. Ask for the audit you will be expected to pass: BSCI, Sedex, SMETA or equivalent, and ask to see the most recent report, including any findings.

On the day, look at working hours recorded on the board, whether overtime is voluntary, whether workers have written contracts, and whether the dormitory conditions are acceptable if workers live on site. Facilities that have been audited by a major retailer usually have a visible audit certificate; one that refuses to show you its latest report is telling you something.

The questions that separate a factory from a trading company

Ask these casually during a walk-through. The answers matter more than the tour.

Which machine runs this step? Can I watch it run? Who sets up the changeover between orders? Where is the tooling stored, and is it marked with my name? If I move this order elsewhere next year, what happens to the mould?

A trading company gives you a showroom tour and deflects all of these. A factory answers without hesitation because the answers are on the floor, visible from where you are standing.

What to do with the findings

An audit is not a pass or fail. It produces a list, and you decide which items matter. A minor finding on dormitory signage is not worth walking away from a factory that is otherwise strong. A finding on no quality records, or production subcontracted elsewhere, is a reason to stop.

Put the significant findings in writing as conditions in the purchase order. An audit report that nobody acts on is the same as no audit, and it is worth remembering that the report is only useful if the contract refers to it.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a factory audit in China cost?

Typically US$200 to 400 per inspector per day for a drinkware or general merchandise factory, covering travel, accommodation and the written report. Many suppliers will allow an independent auditor at the buyer's cost. It is usually split 50/50 between buyer and supplier if you agree it in advance.

Can I audit a factory myself by video call?

A video call is useful for a first pass and can expose inconsistencies, but it cannot verify capacity, labour conditions or traceability. Treat it as a screening step, not a substitute. If a supplier declines an on-site audit but offers video, ask why and what the concern is.

What if the factory refuses a third-party audit?

Treat it as significant information. A supplier confident in its quality has nothing to object to. Refusal usually indicates the goods are not ready, production is subcontracted somewhere you have not seen, or the order is not what was quoted. You can proceed knowingly, but you should not proceed believing otherwise.

Does an ISO 9001 certificate mean the factory is good?

It means the quality management system is documented and audited, which is a meaningful baseline. It does not mean every product is good, and certificates are sometimes issued to entities that do not manufacture. Ask for the certificate in the exact company name on your contract, and verify it independently.
